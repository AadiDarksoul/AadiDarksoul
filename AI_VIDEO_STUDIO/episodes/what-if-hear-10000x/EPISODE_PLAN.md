### What they do well (keep these)
1. **Second-person narration ("You…")** in short, punchy, declarative lines. The viewer *is* the character.
2. **Cold open on the power's most visual demo**, before any explanation (battery, bullet, cameras).
3. **"Amazing… and awful"**: every perk is paid for with a cost. This is the engine of the whole format.
4. **Setup → payoff callbacks**: "you laugh a beat late on purpose" pays off with "you laughed right on time". Comments reward these with timestamps.
5. **Specific numbers** make it feel real: 400 m/s, 250 km/h, 300 names, 3.2 billion.
6. **Consistent cheap style**: black stickmen with **one hoodie colour as identity** (Leo blue, Max red), flat vector rooms, HUD/data panels ("HITS 158", "FACE DETECTED – NOT IDENTIFIED"), chalk-style brain diagrams, CCTV frames, warm practical lights on a navy palette.
7. **New composition roughly every 5 seconds** (storyboard tiles almost never repeat).
8. **Serial cliffhangers + cross-linked parts** ("Part 2 of 3", final-shot twist).

### Where they are lacking (our gaps to fill)

## PART B — Our episode concept

**Character:** **Kai**: black stickman, **orange hoodie**, and **huge over-ear noise-cancelling headphones he is never seen without** (identity prop + story device; the headphones coming off = the climax).

**Supporting:** **Tara** (8), the kid upstairs who taps a rhythm on her floor every night to annoy him: *tap-tap-tap … tap… tap* (3 short, 2 long). **Mr. Okafor**, the rescue captain.

**The 3 rules (stated in Act 1, never broken):**
1. Kai hears sounds **10,000× quieter** than normal (40 dB below human threshold). His loudness range is NOT bigger, so loud sounds hurt even more.
2. He can **focus** on one sound, but only by first letting *everything* in.
3. Every full "open" damages his ears a little. Too long = permanent damage.

**Through-line (open loop from frame 1):** in the hook he hears a whisper say his name through a wall. The payoff at the climax: it was Tara's habit of whispering "Kai, you awake?" to the ceiling, which sets up how he recognises her under the rubble.

**Part 2 cliffhanger:** the earthquake had a **rhythm**. Earthquakes don't have rhythms.

## PART D — Full script (narration = VO, dialogue in quotes)

### 0:00 · COLD OPEN — "Someone said your name"
*(as in Part C)*

**VO:** You can hear a coffee grinder two streets away. A phone buzzing in a stranger's pocket. A fly changing its mind.
**VO:** And through three walls, someone just whispered your name.
*[SILENCE] [TITLE]*

### 0:15 · THE RULES — "Ten thousand times quieter"
**VO:** Normal ears can hear down to about zero decibels. That's a mosquito three metres away, in a silent room.
**VO:** Yours go forty decibels lower. Ten thousand times quieter.
*Visual: dB ladder: rock concert 110 → whisper 30 → mosquito 0 → YOU −40. A tiny stickman falls down the ladder.*
**VO:** Here's the crazy part: your ears were already almost perfect. At their quietest, your eardrum moves less than the width of an atom.
*Visual: eardrum, zoom ×1,000,000, a hydrogen atom next to it.*
**VO:** Push them ten thousand times further… and you discover something nobody else knows.
**VO:** Silence was never silent.
*SFX: a soft, endless hiss fades in.*
**VO:** That hiss? It's air. Billions of air molecules crashing into your eardrums every second. It never stops. Not for one second of your life.
**"Kai, why do you always wear those?"**
**"Because the air is too loud."**
**"…The air."**
**"The air."**

### 1:05 · AMAZING — "You hear everything"
**VO:** So, what's life like at ten thousand times? Amazing.
**VO:** You know who's at the door before they knock. Everyone walks differently. Your mum drags her left heel. The delivery guy jogs. Your landlord… tiptoes when he's about to raise the rent.
*Visual: three footstep waveforms with names.*
**VO:** You hear cars around corners before you see them. You've never been surprised by a bus in your life.
**VO:** People type their passwords in front of you all the time. Every key makes a slightly different click.
*Visual: phone keypad, each key's click shown as a different-coloured ring.*
**VO:** That one's real, by the way. Researchers trained a computer to steal typed passwords just by listening, with about 95% accuracy. You do it without trying. You just… don't.
**VO:** And music? Everyone else listens in black and white. You hear the guitarist's fingers slide. The singer's breath. The drummer's chair creak.
**"Isn't this song amazing?"**
**"The bassist has a cold."**
**VO:** And lies. A lie is hard work. The heart speeds up, breathing changes, the throat tightens before the words come out.
**"Did you do your homework?"**
**"Yes."** *(SFX: heartbeat thump-thump-THUMP)*
**"…You didn't."**
**"I hate you."**
**VO:** You are the world's worst person to lie to. And the world's worst person to throw a surprise party for.

### 2:30 · AWFUL — "Your body is a factory"
**VO:** Now the bad news. The loudest thing you will ever hear… is you.
*Sound design: switch to "inside Kai" audio: low, wet, rhythmic.*
**VO:** Your heart is a drum in your chest. Your blood rushes like a river behind your ears. When you chew, it's an earthquake. When you move your eyes…
*SFX: a tiny creak.*
**VO:** …you can hear your eyes move.
**VO:** That sounds made up. It isn't. People with a rare inner-ear condition called superior canal dehiscence hear exactly this: their own eyeballs moving, their heartbeat, their footsteps booming in their head.
**VO:** And because your ears are ten thousand times more sensitive but your pain limit isn't, loud sounds don't just get louder. They get violent.
*Visual: a fire-truck siren rendered as a giant jagged ring smashing through the frame; Kai doubled over.*
**VO:** A siren is a punch. A dropped plate is a gunshot. Restaurants are war zones.

**VO:** To you, they're the only door you can close.

## PART E — Sound design spec (our differentiator)

**Mix targets:** dialogue/VO about −16 LUFS short-term; master **−14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP**, which matches YouTube normalisation, so we aren't turned down. Stereo 48 kHz.

**Two audio "worlds":**
| World | When | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Outside Kai** | wide shots, other characters' POV | Normal, slightly muffled (low-pass ~8 kHz), mono-ish |
| **Kai's ears** | any time we see sound rings | Full bandwidth, extra air (+3 dB at 10 kHz), **binaural / 3D panning**, tiny details very loud (fly wings, keyboard clicks, fabric) |

**Three planned silences:** 0:11 (before the title), 4:50 (before the collapse), 6:55 (2 s of true silence). No music, no room tone, nothing. Silence is the loudest effect in an episode about hearing.

**Music (BGM):**
- Built **around a heartbeat pulse**. Tempo tracks tension: 60 bpm calm → 90 bpm quake → 120 bpm climax → stops dead at the headphones-off moment.
- **Leitmotif:** Tara's rhythm (3 short + 2 long) as a soft piano/pluck figure. It appears 3×: Act 1 (introduce), climax (it *becomes* the diegetic tapping), end credits.
- Music OFF under every punchline (jokes need air) and at all three silences.
- Mood: ambient synth pads + felt piano; no big orchestral "trailer" hits except one at the collapse.

**Key SFX list:** irregular heartbeat (lie), café walla, coffee grinder (distant), phone vibration, fly wings, keyboard clicks (pitched per key), air hiss (pink noise, very soft), internal body (blood whoosh, chew crunch, *eye creak*), siren (distorted, clipped), plate smash, spoon taps (wood, through the ceiling = low-passed), sub-bass earth groan (25–40 Hz), building collapse, generators/drills, megaphone, tinnitus ring (~6–8 kHz, slowly rising), machine pulse BOOM every 4 s.

**Free sources:** Freesound.org (CC0 filter), Pixabay SFX, YouTube Audio Library. Our studio's own procedural generators also work (heartbeat, drone, riser, stinger, whoosh, wind, thunder are in `AI_VIDEO_STUDIO/studio/synth.py`).

## PART F — Animation & visual style spec

- **Base style:** keep the flat-vector stickman look (audiences expect it, it's fast to make), but make it ours:
  - Kai = orange hoodie + oversized headphones (silhouette readable at thumbnail size).
  - **Sound rings** as the signature visual. Colour-coded: **red = heartbeats, white = voices, yellow = machines, blue = nature, orange = Kai's own body**. Ring speed = loudness, ring spacing = pitch.
  - **Building cross-sections** (dollhouse cutaway) whenever sound travels through walls. This is a unique visual the references don't use.
  - **Audio HUD** instead of their stats panels: a small dB meter + `SOURCE: HEART · 112 BPM` tags. Use it sparingly, ~1 per minute.
- **Palette:** desaturated navy/grey world; only the sound rings are saturated, so the eye always goes to sound. Warm practical light in Tara's flat (emotional colour), cold white floodlights at the rescue.
- **Camera:** slow push-ins on emotional lines; fast pull-back "zoom through walls" transitions (3–4 in the video); one long 2-second hold on the hands on the headphones.
- **Shot rhythm:** new composition every **3–5 s**, a pattern interrupt (style change: diagram, cross-section, HUD close-up, joke cutaway) every **20–40 s**.
- **Infographic beats:** dB ladder, atom-vs-eardrum zoom, keyboard-click colours, animal facts. Same chalk-on-navy look for all science moments, so viewers learn "this look = real fact".

## Fact sources to double-check before publishing
- Human hearing threshold ≈ 0 dB SPL; 10,000× intensity = 40 dB.
- At threshold the eardrum moves on the order of 10⁻¹¹ m, smaller than an atom's diameter (common physiology figure).
- Acoustic keystroke attack: Durham/Surrey/Royal Holloway study (2023), ~95% accuracy from a nearby phone mic.
- Superior canal dehiscence: hearing your own eye movements, heartbeat, footsteps (autophony).
- Rescue "silence" periods with acoustic listening devices: widely reported after the 2023 Türkiye–Syria earthquakes.
- Greater wax moth: hearing up to ~300 kHz (2013 study).
- Elephants: detect seismic/low-frequency vibrations partly through their feet.
- Microsoft Building 87 anechoic chamber: ~−20.6 dBA (Guinness record, 2015).
